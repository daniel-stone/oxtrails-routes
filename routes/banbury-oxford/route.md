A near-flat, easy-to-follow ride along the **Oxford Canal** towpath, from the centre of Banbury to the centre of Oxford. Route finding is easy — just keep to the towpath. Start in Banbury town centre, a short ride from the railway station; the canal soon leaves the town and heads into the Cherwell Valley, with the railway line a constant companion (make a diversion to see the Souldern viaduct).

## Route notes

The towpath is mostly single-track and non-technical — a hybrid or gravel bike will be fine. Some parts are narrow, eroded or overgrown, so if you aren't completely confident in your balance there will be some hike-a-bike. Avoid the route after heavy rain: if the river Cherwell is in flood everything will be very wet, and the railway, canal and river mean there are a few stretches with no easy way back to a road.

There are no shops right on the route, but there are water points and facilities at many of the moorings (Aynho, Lower Heyford, Kings Ground, Thrupp) — though they may be locked (get a key from the Canal &amp; River Trust) or in use. The one cafe directly on the route is Annie's at Thrupp, which is well worth the stop.

As you get closer to Oxford the towpath becomes paved and busy. If it's too crowded to cycle, cross on one of the bridges and ride through the city centre. The route ends a short distance from Oxford railway station.
