One of the oldest roads in the UK — a Bronze Age trade route that follows the high ground of the **North Wessex Downs**, passing ancient monuments and open farmland. The route starts at Goring railway station (you can skip the ride to the start by catching a regular local train). Once over the Thames, a short stretch of road leads to the climb onto the hills. The first 6 miles or so are on nicely surfaced gravel farm tracks; as you approach the A34 the surface deteriorates and can be muddy after wet periods. West of the A34 the terrain is more mixed — grass, gravel and heavily eroded farm tracks, with some sections worn down to the chalk that get very slippery in the wet. Be especially careful on the descent west of Sparsholt Firs.

## Route notes

Do stop at: the Hugh Frederick Grosvenor Memorial, Scutchamer Knob, Lord Wantage Monument, Segsbury Camp, Uffington Castle and the White Horse. The route ends at a National Trust car park — pick your own way home, as Didcot and Swindon railway stations are about the same distance away.

The Ridgeway National Trail is well signposted, so navigation is straightforward. There are very few options for water: there is a tap hidden in a hedge near Hill Barn (SU 337 855), but drink at your own risk. There are no cafes or toilets along the route, and little shelter from the weather — you will be riding into the prevailing wind, so dress appropriately. There are small shops in Uffington, Childrey, Letcombe Regis and Lambourne, but reaching them adds several miles and around 150 m of climbing to the day.
